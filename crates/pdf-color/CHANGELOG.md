# Changelog

## 0.1.0-alpha.1 (unreleased)

The first release, and an alpha: until a release says it's stable, any alpha may change the API, each saying here what changed and how code follows. A crate that depends on an alpha names it exactly, `=0.1.0-alpha.1`: cargo's default, `0.1.0-alpha.1`, takes any later alpha of 0.1.0 too. The crate's own tests don't build from its package, as they read files through pdf-structure, which isn't published (below).

- **Components as PDFium counts them.** `components`, over any resolver, as of chromium/8125, of the value a `/ColorSpace` dictionary holds under a name, as written, or an inline image's array: `Some(n)`, 3 where PDFium loads no space, or none for a reference to nothing, for which it has no space at all. `counted` gives the same as a `Counted`, with whether a cap ended a branch of it. A device name, its abbreviation or `Pattern` by name, and no other family; an array of one by what it holds; an array of more by the first four bytes of its family, a name's or a string's, each family as PDFium loads it; an alternate space by a path of its own, which also takes a stream, by the first of its values in the order of its keys that names a stock space, and doesn't go into an array of one. A Pattern space counts its underlying space's and the pattern's name, up to 16. References are followed as far as PDFium does, and a loop doesn't load.
- **Numbers as PDFium's accessors read them.** `/N`, `/FunctionType`, `/BitsPerSample` and `/Size`'s elements take a real cut from its 32-bit float, `true` as 1, an integer past 32 bits as 0 and anything else as 0; a white point's take a number's 32-bit float, and `Y` is compared as one.
- **A function's shape, by its dictionary alone.** What a DeviceN space asks of its tint transform: how many inputs and outputs it has, or that PDFium refuses it, for each of the four kinds, no stream decoded and nothing evaluated. So a sampled function shorter than its sizes say and a PostScript function that doesn't parse count as loading, where PDFium's don't: `tests/pdfium/differ.tsv` holds them to it.
- **The spaces, typed, as ISO 32000-2's 8.6 says them.** `Space::read`, over any resolver: `Family`, `Space` and its `#[non_exhaustive]` structs `Cie`, `Icc`, `Indexed`, `Separation`, `DeviceN`, `Attributes` and `Process`, `Lookup` and `Subtype`, with `Space::family`, `components`, `base`, `alternate` and `colorants`. `Icc::id` and `Lookup::Stream`'s `id` are a stream's number, what a reader asks for its data by. All of it borrows from the file. No conversion, no profile opened, no function evaluated, no stream decoded. A name that's no family is no space: `Spaces` is what looks one up.
- **Colorants.** `Colorants`, an iterator over a Separation space's one or a DeviceN space's names, each through its references, fused, its `next` and `size_hint` hints to inline, as `core`'s own adapters' are; and `Colorant::of`, what a name says of itself: `Cyan`, `Magenta`, `Yellow`, `Black`, `All`, `None` or `Spot`. `Attributes::separations` reads a `/Colorants` dictionary's spaces on asking, each a `ColorantSpace`. `DeviceN::names` and `Process::components` hold each element by its position, none for one that's no name.
- **`Fault` and `Sink`.** Each departure, a `thiserror` value with its clause, told to a sink, pdf-objects' `fault::Sink<Fault>`, with its `fault::Place`: the numbered object it's in, by pdf-objects' `ObjectId`, and where in it it was written. They're the types pdf-semantics' and pdf-fonts' faults are told to, so one sink can take all three; a sink of one's own implements `Sink<pdf_color::Fault>`, naming the trait through `pdf_color::pdf_objects::fault` at the version this crate takes, and a bound on one is `S: Sink<Fault> + ?Sized`. A `Vec<(Place, Fault)>` keeps them, and `()` wants none. Reading goes on past each. What only a check reads is behind `Sink::wants`, and spends a budget of its own, so the same space is given whatever the sink, an `Err` included, `()` pays for no check, and a read a check makes is never the `Err`: damaged, it's told, as `Fault::Damaged` with the object's `ObjectId`, and any other failure leaves the check unsaid. References are followed through, each lookup a visit, a chain told as `Chained`, a loop as `Loop`, the caps as `TooDeep` and `TooManyVisits`.
- **One promise between the two readings.** A space read with a sink that wants faults, none told, counts by `Space::components` as `components` counts it, where `counted` meets no cap, but for a Pattern space over more than 16 components, which no clause refuses and PDFium does: the fuzz target and every case in `tests/pdfium` hold it. So a white point above 0 that PDFium's 32-bit reading makes 0 is told, past ISO 32000-2.
- **What a resources dictionary names.** `Spaces::read` finds its `/ColorSpace` dictionary, a stream's among them, `/Resources` and `/ColorSpace` each through at most two references, as PDFium: past that, or in a loop, none, and no error, a fault told of none of it. `Spaces::iter` and `Spaces::get` give each `Named` (`#[non_exhaustive]`): the key as the reader read it, where it was written and what it holds, a key written twice at its last entry and an empty key no name. `Spaces::iter` gives them in order as an `Iter`, a type a caller can name and hold, read from either end, its upper bound the dictionary's entries.
- **A resources dictionary's spaces, counted.** `Spaces::components` gives each name as `Spaces::iter` does, with what `counted` gives for its value under the `Limits` given, as a `Components`: each name counted as it's asked for, nothing kept between them, its upper bound the dictionary's entries, so a caller can make room for every name at once. It allocates what `Spaces::iter` does, pdf-objects' set of the dictionary's keys past 32 entries, made once, and what each name's `counted` does, a stack of the objects a reference leads it through, at most `space_depth` and one, let go as the name ends. A name whose space can't be read has that `Err` for its count, and the names after it are counted all the same; a caller that wants the whole as one `Err` stops at the first by `?`. It takes any resolver that's `Copy` (`&reading`, `&document`, a closure of references), and its `Debug` gives the names it has left, as `Iter`'s does, and its caps, but not the resolver, which a closure can't print, as `core`'s adapters leave out their closures. The fuzz target holds its first 32 names, on any input, to `Spaces::iter`'s in order, each with what `counted` gives for its value, or a failure for the same reason, and its upper bound to `Spaces::iter`'s.
- **Caps.** `Limits` (`#[non_exhaustive]`), `Limits::DEFAULT`: how deep a space nests, 64, and how many objects are looked up for one, and subfunctions written in place read, 4,096 per space, and as many again for what a typed space's checks read, where each name kept and each `/Colorants` entry read spends one too. Past one, a branch doesn't load, so a chain of arrays of one by reference past 64 counts otherwise than PDFium's, which has only its stack, and short of them a value nested in place past where PDFium's parser stops can too: `tests/pdfium/differ.tsv` holds each.
- **A failed read.** An object that's `Why::Damaged` isn't there, as for PDFium, and any other failure to read one is the `Err`, whole, through `components` and `Spaces::read` alike.
- **pdf-objects, re-exported whole.** `pdf_color::pdf_objects` is the pdf-objects this crate is built with, so a caller names the values read (`Object`, `ObjectId`), the resolver (`Resolve`), a failed read (`Unreadable`, `Why`), where something was written (`Span`), `dict!` and a sink of its own through it, at the version this crate takes, with no dependency of its own, or as pdf-objects' own where it depends on pdf-objects itself. None of pdf-objects' items is re-exported by name: a crate whose API names another family crate's types re-exports that crate whole, and only its own types by name, so a type is never reached by two paths that can part at a version.
- **Errors, and hashing.** `Fault` derives `Display` and `std::error::Error` with thiserror, with no `source`; a failed read is pdf-objects' `Unreadable`, whole. `Family`, `Subtype`, `Colorant`, `Fault` and `Counted` are `Hash`, so a caller can key a map by a family, a fault or a count, or tally them. `Limits` isn't, as a cap is no key; nor are `Space`, the structs of its parameters, `Lookup`, `Named` and `Spaces`, which hold pdf-objects' values, reals among them, with no `Eq` for a hash to agree with.
- **Marked, and `const` where it can be.** `#[must_use]` is on every function whose result dropped is a mistake and whose type doesn't say so itself: `Family::of`, `name`, `is_device`, `is_cie` and `is_special`; `Colorant::of` and `is_process`; `Space::family`, `components`, `base` and `alternate`; and `Spaces::get`. The iterators, `Colorants`, `Iter` and `Components`, which give nothing until asked, are marked themselves, so `Space::colorants`, `Spaces::iter` and `Spaces::components` are by what they give. None is on what gives a `Result`. `Family::of`, `name`, `is_device`, `is_cie` and `is_special`, `Colorant::of` and `is_process`, and `Space::family` are `const fn`s, so what a name says, and a space's family, can be had in a constant. Every function that gives a `Result` says what fails it and when, under `# Errors`: `components`, `counted`, `Space::read`, `Spaces::read` and `Attributes::separations`; and `Spaces::components` and `Components` say what fails a name's count.
- **`#[non_exhaustive]`, and said.** Every enum, `Family`, `Space`, `Lookup`, `Subtype`, `Colorant` and `Fault`, and every struct whose fields are public, `Cie`, `Icc`, `Indexed`, `Separation`, `DeviceN`, `Attributes`, `Process`, `ColorantSpace`, `Named`, `Counted` and `Limits`, is `#[non_exhaustive]`, each doc saying why: a family, a subtype, what a colorant's name says or a fault can join one in a minor release, as a later edition of the standard can name one or a file shows one, and a struct can hold a part more, so a match on one needs a wildcard and a struct is made by the crate (`Limits` on a copy of `Limits::DEFAULT`). `Lookup` is, though it's the two forms 8.6.6.3 gives a table, as making it exhaustive later is a minor release, where the other way round would be a breaking one; `Lookup::Stream` is too. `Fault`'s variants aren't: each field is the whole of what it says, a position or an object, so a fault is built by a literal, as a test compares with it, and one that says more joins as a variant of its own. `Spaces`, `Colorants`, `Iter` and `Components` keep their fields to themselves. The README says so.
- **Benchmarks.** `cargo bench -p pdf-color` times the count, the typed reading and a resources dictionary's spaces, on inputs the benchmarks make as they run, the same on every run: `counted`, and `Space::read` with `()` and with a sink that keeps every fault, of a space of each kind a resources dictionary or an inline image holds (DeviceGray by name, DeviceRGB in an array of one, CalRGB, ICCBased, Indexed, Separation, an NChannel DeviceN space with its attributes, Pattern over DeviceRGB, and DeviceN whose tint transform stitches eight functions by reference); a `/ColorSpace` dictionary of 8, 64 and 1,024 names found by `Spaces::read`, each name counted, as a reader of content counts a page's, by `counted` and again by `Spaces::components`; and a DeviceN space's 32 colorants, each by `Colorant::of`. Each input is held to what it gives before it's timed: a count to PDFium's, with no cap met; a typed space to its family, its components and no fault; a dictionary to each name's count, in order; the colorants to their tally. Throughput is in spaces, or in names or colorants. So a change is judged before and after it. The harness is criterion, a dev-dependency never built for a user of the crate, and `benches/` is outside the package.
- **The package, checked before a publish.** CI packages the crate as a publish would (`cargo publish --dry-run`, in a target directory of its own made fresh each time, so a stale build can't pass or fail it) and checks what the package holds: `src/`, the README, the CHANGELOG, the manifest and the lock, nothing else, no benchmark among them. CI tests the crate on its `rust-version`. The manifest names the crate's documentation (docs.rs). The README's licence links are the repository's URLs, which reach the files from crates.io and docs.rs, and a test holds them so. The tests read files through pdf-structure, which isn't published: it's a dev-dependency by its path alone, which the package's manifest leaves out, so the crate packages and publishes on pdf-objects alone, and its own unit tests don't build from the package.
- **Where it comes from.** Written for pdf-content-streams, a reader of content, which sizes an unfiltered inline image by its space's components to find where its data ends, and for pdf-lint, a PDF checker, whose color checks read each space typed; no release before this one had it, pdf-lint 0.1.0's included. PDFium's count is its behaviour as of chromium/8125, followed from PDFium's source as a reference and written here in this crate's own code and words, each reading a case asked of PDFium's build, its answer recorded (`tests/pdfium`). The typed reading is ISO 32000-2's 8.6, read in its clauses and written in this crate's words.
- **Tests.** Unit tests for PDFium's count of each family by name and in an array, a function of each kind and what each refuses, numbers as its accessors read them, a family by its first four bytes, references followed once, loops, the caps at and past them, and a failed read; the typed reading of each family well-formed, each fault by its clause and where it's told, reading on past each, what only a check reads under every sink, each reading's visits its own, the promise between the readings and its one exception, colorants and separations; a resources dictionary through its two references, its names in order and from either end, counted, a failed and a capped one among them; what a name says, in a constant; and the README's licence links. `tests/pdfium` holds 524 cases, each a file whose page draws an inline image in a space its resources name, to PDFium's recorded answer over a reading and over a document, each the crate counts otherwise listed in `differ.tsv` with why, and each space read with no fault told to the promise; `just test-pdfium` asks PDFium again. A fuzz target, `just fuzz color`, on values and resources dictionaries of any shape, mutated from fixtures of every family and from random bytes, and on files: each count within every cap and the same under other caps where none was met, each typed space the same whatever the sink and counting as it was counted where no fault was told, each of a resources dictionary's first 32 names counted as it's given as it's counted alone, and a file's every object read alike over a reading and its document; three unit tests run its body.
