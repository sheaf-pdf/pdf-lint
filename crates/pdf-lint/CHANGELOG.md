# Changelog

## 0.2.0 (unreleased)

A breaking release, of the library above all, and one whose reports move. 0.1.0 read what it couldn't decode or decrypt itself through qpdf; this release reads, decodes and decrypts every file itself, and checks more of it, each under rules of its own: content, page trees, name and number trees, XMP metadata, color spaces, fonts, what object streams hold, earlier revisions and linearization. Readings 0.1.0 had wrong are mended, each entry saying how reports move where they do. What 0.1.0's library held besides the checker, the objects, the reader and the Arlington model with its predicates, is in crates of their own, which this one re-exports whole: the first entry says where each of 0.1.0's paths went, and the entries after it name an item by the crate that has it, `pdf_lint::pdf_objects`, `pdf_lint::pdf_structure` or `pdf_lint::pdf_arlington`. What each of those holds is its own CHANGELOG's; the entries here say what a user of 0.1.0 meets.

- **Breaking, the library: the objects, the reader and the model are crates of their own, re-exported whole.** The root holds pdf-lint's own items alone, `check`, `check_with_reading`, `file_version`, `grammar()`, `use_model`, `Options`, `Report`, `Finding`, `Rule`, `Hold`, `Made`, `Exempt`, `Class`, `Unchecked`, `Unlisted`, `UnlistedIn`, `Linearized`, `Allowance`, `Allowances` and the errors of their parsers (below), and the crates whose types those take and give, each whole, at the version pdf-lint takes: `pdf_lint::pdf_objects`, `pdf_lint::pdf_structure` (pdf-crypt and pdf-filters under it, `pdf_structure::pdf_crypt` and `::pdf_filters`) and `pdf_lint::pdf_arlington`. Each of 0.1.0's other paths is where its crate has it: `object`, `text` and `version` are pdf-objects' modules (`pdf_objects::object`, `::text`, `::version`), and `Object`, `ObjectId`, `Span`, `Dict`, `Entry`, `Value`, `Str`, `Stream`, `Version` and the `dict!` macro at its root; `bytes` is pdf-structure's `read` and `source` its `source`, and `Source`, `Document`, `Trailer` and `Update` at its root; `predicate` and `COMMIT` are pdf-arlington's (`pdf_arlington::predicate`, `::COMMIT`), as is `grammar`'s model, its types and `corrections`, `additions`, `version` and `HEADER` (`pdf_arlington::grammar`), and `Grammar`, `Table`, `Row`, `At`, `ArlType`, `Wrap`, `WrapFn`, `Wrapped`, `Cx`, `Truth`, `Reference`, `Since` and `Gate` at its root; and `extension::ISO` is `pdf_arlington::extension::ISO`. `grammar` keeps what's pdf-lint's, `grammar()`, `use_model`, `NAMED` and `Refused`, and `extension` keeps `declared` and `named`; `check` and `allowance` keep their items. `qpdf`, `Qpdf` and `Warning` are gone (below). pdf-objects and pdf-structure are MIT OR Apache-2.0, and pdf-arlington Apache-2.0, as the model is; each is an alpha, which pdf-lint takes at its exact version. pdf-objects re-exports pdf-tokenizer 0.2 (`pdf_objects::pdf_tokenizer`, and its `Rules` as `pdf_objects::Rules`), a breaking release of the one 0.1.0 depended on: a user that names pdf-tokenizer 0.1 too has two of its types until it moves to 0.2. `check::is_date` says whether pdf-semantics' `Date::read` reads a date, built without its `clock` feature, so without chrono; what it takes for a date is unchanged, which a test holds to TestGrammar's `is_valid_pdf_date_string` on a million strings. This package no longer holds the model's files, nor licence files: upstream's LICENSE and NOTICE.txt, which the parts of the checker adapted from TestGrammar are under too, are in pdf-arlington's `model/`, and `src/check.rs`'s header carries the NOTICE's copyright and DARPA notices for those parts. `fuzz`, the fuzz target's body, built for tests and under `--cfg fuzzing` alone, is hidden from the docs.
- **Breaking, the library: qpdf is gone from it, and the reader has one way in.** `Qpdf`, the `qpdf` module and its `OLDEST`, and the reader's `Decode` trait, `Written` and `read_with` are gone, and nothing shipped reads `PDF_LINT_QPDF`. pdf-structure's `read::document(file, &options)` reads a file whole, where `bytes::read`, `read_with` and `read_from` did, as its `read::Options` say: from which cross-reference section (`section`, `read_from`'s), by which passwords an encrypted file is opened (`passwords`, below), how a file whose sections aren't where it says is read (`recovery`), which Flate backend inflates its streams (`flate`) and under which limits (`limits`). `Reading::open(source, &options)` opens a file to read on demand, and `WithReading::new(source, &options)` reads one whole beside its reading (below). `read::NESTING` and `read::SECTIONS` are as they were; `read::XrefEntry` is `#[non_exhaustive]`, as `XrefEntry::Free` is, so each can grow without a breaking release. Every iterator the reader and a `Document` give is a type of its own (`read::Ids`, `source::Objects` and the rest).
- **Breaking, the library: room to grow, and every item documented.** `Rule`, `Finding`, `Report`, `Options`, `Unlisted`, `Unchecked` and `Allowance` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, as the new `Linearized` is, so a rule, a field or a verdict can come without a breaking release. Outside the crate, a match on `Rule` or `Linearized` takes a wildcard; a `Finding`, an `Unlisted`, an `Unchecked` and an `Allowance` are only a check's or `Allowances::parse`'s to make, read by their fields, a pattern that names their fields ending in `..`; a `Report` starts from `Report::default()`; and `Options` from `Options::default()`, its fields set on it, where a literal of it, `..Options::default()` or not, no longer builds. `Made`, `Hold`, `UnlistedIn`, `Class` and `Exempt` stay exhaustive on purpose, each type's doc saying why. `Rule::ALL` is `&[Rule]` and `grammar::NAMED` `&[&str]`, not arrays of a fixed length (0.1's were `[Rule; 14]` and `[&str; 4]`), so a rule or a table the checker reads by name can come without a breaking release; `use_model` refuses a model without an `ObjectStream` or a `LinearizationParameterDict` table, which it took. `Options` has `unused`, `departures`, `content_budget` and `font_budget` more, `Report` `content`, `unused`, `departures` and `linearized`, and `Finding` `revision`, each said below. Every public item is documented, held by the `missing_docs` lint.
- **Breaking, the library: typed errors.** `Allowances::read` gives an `allowance::ReadError`, `Allowances::parse` an `AllowanceError`, `Hold::parse` a `HoldError`, `Exempt::parse` an `ExemptError` and `use_model` a `grammar::Refused`, where each gave an `anyhow::Error`; and pdf-arlington's `Grammar::load` and `predicate::parse` a `grammar::Error` and a `predicate::Error`. Each derives `Display` and `std::error::Error` with thiserror, `#[non_exhaustive]`, a variant for each thing that can be wrong, with its line or path. Its `Display` is the whole message, as the command line prints it; and none has a `source`: a std error under one, reading a file or a number that doesn't parse, is a field, said after `: `. `AllowanceError`, `HoldError` and `ExemptError` are at the root too. anyhow is the command line's alone.
- **A `Finding`'s and a `Report`'s `Debug` hold none of the file's text.** A finding's value, which quotes the file's names, strings and content, and its path, whose step in a name or number tree is the tree's key, are shown by their lengths in bytes, and a report's paths by their count, where they were shown as they are, strings among them, which may be decrypted. The rest is as it was, a report's findings shown as a finding is; its `Display`, the report as the command line prints it, is unchanged. A finding is compared by `==` or by its fields.
- **What's a mistake to drop is `#[must_use]`, and some functions `const`.** A call that drops the answer of `check`, `check_with_reading`, `file_version`, `grammar()`, `extension::named`, `extension::declared`, `Report::objects`, `beyond`, `since` and `by_path`, `Rule::name` and `Rule::named`, `Made::name`, `UnlistedIn::name` and `UnlistedIn::named`, `Linearized::name`, `Class::of`, `check::is_date`, `check::numberless`, `Allowances::allows` or `Allowances::unused` warns. `Rule::name`, `Made::name`, `UnlistedIn::name` and `Linearized::name` are `const fn`.
- **`Allowances::rows` gives an `allowance::Rows`,** an iterator of its own type over the allowances in their file's order, where it gave an `impl Iterator`: so it can be named in a signature and held in a struct; it's `Clone`, walks from either end (`DoubleEndedIterator`), knows how many are left (`ExactSizeIterator`) and gives none after its last (`FusedIterator`). A loop over it reads as before.
- **Breaking, the library: ids are pdf-objects' struct.** `ObjectId` is pdf-objects' `struct ObjectId { number, generation }`, where it was the pair `(u32, u16)`: `Finding::object`, `Hold::Only`'s map and a `Span`'s stream hold it, and a library user's `(1, 0)` is `ObjectId::new(1, 0)`, `id.0` `id.number`. The reports don't move: the JSON's `[n, g]` and the text's `n g R` are written as they were.
- **Breaking, the library: a string keeps its form.** `Value::String` holds the string's `Form`, `Literal` or `Hex`, beside its `Str`: `Value::String(Str, Option<Form>)`, so a pattern `Value::String(s)` is `Value::String(s, _)` now. The reader gives each string the form it was written in, a decrypted one too; a string built by `Object::bytes` has none, and `Object::hex` makes a hex one. `Form` is at pdf-objects' root. `fn:IsHexString` reads it, unknown for a string without one, so it answers as before; the `hex` method of `Source` and the `hex` fields of `Document` and `Cx`, which gave it by a string's place, are gone.
- **Breaking, the library: `Object::string` is gone.** It kept a Rust string's UTF-8 bytes as a byte string, which read back as a text string (ISO 32000-2, 7.9.2.2) only when the text was ASCII: `é` read back as `Ã©`. `Object::text`, new, writes a text string that reads back as given, literal where PDFDocEncoding holds it and hex as UTF-16BE; `Object::bytes` is a string of bytes as they are. For ASCII, `Object::text` writes the bytes `Object::string` wrote.
- **Breaking, the library: an edit drops a value's span.** `Object`'s value is private, read by `value()` and taken by `into_value()`; `value_mut()`, `as_dict_mut()`, `as_array_mut()` and `as_stream_mut()` change it, each an edit, which drops its span, and the span of each value that holds it, so a span says where a value was written only while no edit has changed it. `as_written_mut()` keeps the span, for changing a value into what the same bytes stand for, as the reader does when it decrypts a string. A `Stream`'s `data` is a `Data`, where it was a span: the reader gives `Data::At(span)`, which `data.at()` reads; `Data::Bytes` holds a stream's bytes in memory (`Stream::new`, `set_bytes`). `Data` is at pdf-objects' root. `Stream` is `#[non_exhaustive]`, made outside the crate by `Stream::new` or `Object::stream`, a pattern that names its fields ending in `..`; `Value::Stream` holds a `Box<Stream>`, which a pattern can't see through, so `Value::Stream(Stream { dict, .. })` is `Value::Stream(s)` and `s.dict` now. `Dict::remove` gives the value it removed, so a closure that calls it and returns its value no longer returns `()`. And new: `as_bool`, `as_bytes`, `as_array` and `as_stream`; an `Object` from an `ObjectId`, an `f32`, a `String` (a name) and the smaller integers; `Dict::get_mut`, `iter_mut`, `has_type`, `type_name`, `remove_duplicates`, `collect` into a `Dict`, and `for` over a `&Dict`. A dictionary's lookups allocate nothing for up to 32 entries.
- **Breaking, the library: `text`'s types read by their fields, and `Span`'s by its methods.** pdf-objects' `text::Text`, `text::Quirk` and `text::Encoding` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a `Text` is read by its fields and isn't built or matched without `..` outside pdf-objects, and a match on `Quirk` or `Encoding` takes a wildcard. A `Text` has `languages` more, the language tags its escapes held, and `Quirk` `UnclosedEscape` and `MalformedEscape`, for an escape that doesn't close or whose tag isn't one. A `Span`'s fields are private: one is made by `Span::new(stream, start, end)` and read by its methods. New, as pdf-objects writes text strings and reads them by PDFium's rules too: `text::encode`, `text::encode_as` and its `Unencodable`, `text::pdfium`, a text string as PDFium reads it, and `text::decode_str`, which reads by either, as pdf-tokenizer's `Rules` (`pdf_objects::Rules`) say.
- **References renumbered, and places dropped.** `Object::renumber(to)` names each reference in a value anew, as `to` names the object it names, or null where it names none: an edit of each reference it changes, dropping the places of it and of what holds it, every other value keeping its own; it says whether it changed any. `Object::unplace` drops every place in a value, a stream's data's where it was written among them (`Data::Unplaced` then), for an object of one file put among another's, whose places would be read as that file's (a `FileCx`'s, say): its stream's data is given first as its bytes. Both are pdf-objects', whose writer names references through a renumbering too (`pdf_objects::serialize`). Every report is unchanged.
- **Breaking, the library: a `Source` is a `Streams`.** pdf-structure's `Source`, which `check` takes, is pdf-objects' `Streams` too (`trait Source: Streams`): a stream's data as a viewer takes it (`Streams::decoded`), beside `decoded_within`, the reader's own account, which the checks read. `ids`, `trailer`, `header_version` and `size` are as they were; `get` gives an `Unread`, where it gave a `String`, and `faults` the reader's faults, where `warnings` gave its warnings (below); `metadata` gives an `Option<Cow<[u8]>>`, which a document lends (`.as_deref()` where an `Option<&[u8]>` was); `hex` is gone (above); and what's new defaults to nothing (below). A `Source` of one's own implements `Streams::decoded` too. pdf-objects' `Streams` and `Resolve`, and `Unreadable` and `Why`, what `Resolve::try_resolve` says of an object that doesn't read, are at its root, so a source or a resolver of one's own names them at the version pdf-lint takes. A file's reading (`Reading`) is a `Source`, answering of what it has read when it's asked; what the command line checks is a file read whole beside its reading (`WithReading`).
- **What a check of a file's structure reads.** pdf-structure's `Revision` is one of a file's revisions, with its trailer, which `Document::revisions` and `Reading::revisions` give. `Source` has `record`, `held` (an object stream's decoded data), `revisions`, `facts` and `in_the_clear`, each defaulting to nothing; `Document::in_the_clear` holds the object streams an encrypted file leaves in the clear while it encrypts its strings. pdf-arlington evaluates `fn:NoCycle`, which the model asks of an object stream's `/Extends`.
- **Breaking, the library: why an object doesn't read is typed, and the reader's types can grow.** `Source::get` gives pdf-structure's `Unread` where it gave a `String`, and `Document::unread` one for each object that doesn't read: `#[non_exhaustive]`, derived with thiserror, a variant for each reason, its `Display` the reason in words; a `Source` of its own says why in its own words with `Unread::Other`. `Trailer`, `Update` and `Encryption::Opened` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, so each can grow without a breaking release, and none can be written as a literal outside pdf-structure: a reader outside the crate makes its trailer by `Trailer::new`, setting `stream` and `unwritten` on it after, its update by `Update::new(objects, previous)` and an opened file's encryption by `Encryption::opened(owner)`, with `.by(..)` for which password opened it; and a pattern that names `Trailer`'s, `Update`'s or `Opened`'s fields ends in `..`. Why the sections don't read (`Trailer::unwritten`) is pdf-structure's `read::Unwritten`, where it was a `String`, its `Display` the words.
- **Breaking, the library: a document's objects through methods.** `Document`'s `objects` field, a public map, is private, each object kept with where the reader read it while it's as read, so a record is always of the object beside it. `contains(id)` says whether an object is listed, read or not, where `objects.contains_key(&id)` did; `objects()` gives each that reads, by number, `range(..)` those in a range of ids, and `unread()` each that doesn't, with why; `Source`'s `ids` and `get` read as before. `set(id, object)` puts one in, with no record, giving back the one it replaces; `insert(n, object)` is as before, `set` at generation 0; `set_unread(id, why)` puts why one doesn't read, where a reader outside the crate inserted an `Err`; `remove(id)` takes one out, its record with it; and `get_mut(id)` gives one to edit, dropping its record, where `objects.get_mut` or `values_mut` did. `--last-update` asks the revision before by `contains`, so its reports are unchanged. A document numbers new objects past every number the file lists (`new_id`, `add`, `max_id`, `set_max_id`), follows references (`dereference`, `get_dict`, `get_dict_mut`, `catalog`, `catalog_mut`), changes its trailer's dictionary (`Trailer::dict_mut`), and walks what its trailer reaches (`reachable`) and removes the rest (`prune`); two documents are equal only where their new ids start at one number too.
- **Breaking, the library: `Document`'s fields private.** A `Document`'s fields are private, read and set by its methods: `doc.trailer` is `doc.trailer()` (`doc.trailer_mut()` to change it), `doc.header` `doc.header_version()`, `doc.warnings` `doc.faults()` (below), `doc.decoded.get(&id)` `doc.held(id)`, `doc.update` `doc.update()`, `doc.size` `Source::size` and `doc.metadata` `doc.metadata()`; what a reader outside pdf-structure fills in is set by `set_header_version`, `set_update`, `set_size`, `set_metadata`, `set_encryption` and `set_revisions`, and `encryption()`, `revisions()`, `facts()` and `in_the_clear(id)` are new. `Document` is `#[non_exhaustive]`, made by `Document::new` as before. An object stream a document holds is lent as its data decoded only while its object is as read, not once it's put, edited, set unread or removed.
- **Breaking, the library: the reader's faults typed.** `Warning` is gone, from `source` and from the root: what the reader had to choose how to read is pdf-structure's `Fault`, kept with its place, pdf-objects' `fault::Place`, where a `Warning` held its `object`, `at` and `message`; pdf-objects is re-exported whole, as `pdf_lint::pdf_objects`, so that `Place` and the `Sink` that `Source::tell_faults` tells (`pdf_lint::pdf_objects::fault::{Place, Sink}`) are named at the version this crate takes. `Source::warnings` is `Source::faults`, `(Place, Fault)` pairs in the reader's order, and a `Document`'s `warnings` field is private, read by `doc.faults()`. `warning.message` is `fault.to_string()`, the fault in words, and `warning.object` and `warning.at` are `place.object` and `place.at`. A `reader` finding's value is the fault's words, and an `unresolved` trailer's the reason's.
- **A reading on demand.** pdf-structure's `Reading` opens a file (`Reading::open(source, &options)`), an encrypted one by the passwords its options give, or the empty password, given none, and one no password opened by others after (`unlock`, given their encodings), and reads each object when it's first asked for, from any thread, from bytes in memory or a file read by position, a window at a time (`FileBytes`, whose `Identity` tells whether it's changed, knowing the file by same-file: by its device and inode on Unix and its volume serial number and file index on Windows, so another file made at its path once it's removed isn't taken for it); read whole, it gives what `read::document` gives (`into_document`, `to_document`, or `WithReading`, the document with its reading kept beside it), by the same code. It hands out any stream's data when it's asked for (`data`), decrypted in an encrypted file that opened as an object stream's is, a metadata stream by its own `/Crypt` filter and an embedded file without one by the crypt filter `/EFF` names (from `/V` 4), refused where it may be ciphertext the reader doesn't decrypt, and decoded through its filters within a limit (`decoded_within`), its error holding pdf-filters' (`pdf_structure::pdf_filters`) where the data doesn't decode cleanly. Its options say which Flate backend its streams inflate on (`Options::flate`): pdf-filters' miniz_oxide by default, which pdf-lint's reading inflates on, or zlib-rs with pdf-structure's `zlib-rs` feature, which pdf-lint doesn't turn on, though its build has zlib-rs all the same, for pdf-content-streams (below). Its limits are a value a caller sets (`read::Limits`, in `read::Options`), the same in a build under cargo-fuzz (`--cfg fuzzing`): the fuzz target sets small ones itself. `read::filters`, `read::encryption` and `read::viewed` give a stream's filters, an encryption dictionary's values and what a viewer takes of a decode, as the reader reads them. A `Document` has where the reader read each object (`record`, `records`) and what the file says of itself as written (`facts()`, its defaults for one built by `Document::new`).
- **Where an object was read from, and whether its file changed.** pdf-structure's `Record` holds the number and generation its object was read as (`Record::id`) and a hash of the bytes it names, private, which a writer checks a copy of them by: two records are equal only where both are too, as two readings of one file still read alike. `ByteSource` has `unchanged`, whether a source's bytes are those it had, true by default, which `FileBytes` answers by its file's length and modification time, forwarded through a reference, a `Box` and an `Arc`. Every report is unchanged.
- **A document taken by another reading.** `Reading::adopt` takes a `Document` read whole (`read::document`, `Reading::into_document`) as a reading of its file opened again reads it, where it reads each object alike, the same record but its own, and the document is encrypted, and opened, as the file is, its encryption dictionary and first `/ID` element the file's, read by a password where the reading was: each record then names that reading, so pdf-structure's writer writes the document through it, its streams and copies among it. Else it leaves the document as it was, and says why (`read::Error::Unlike` for an object, `read::Error::UnlikeEncryption` for the encryption; `read::Error` is `#[non_exhaustive]`). Every report is unchanged.
- **Breaking, the library: a `Cx` is made from a `FileCx`.** pdf-arlington's `predicate::FileCx`, at its root too, is what every row of a file is evaluated in: `FileCx::new(trailer, version, &resolve)`, then `.size(..)` and `.extensions(..)`, once a file; `file.at(container, at)` makes a row's `Cx`, which `.parents(..)` and `.defaults(..)` complete, each giving a new one (`#[must_use]`), and whose `container()` and `at()` read it. `Cx`'s fields are private, so a literal of it no longer builds, and it has two lifetimes where it had one, `Cx<'c, 'a>`, the row's borrow and the file's: `&Cx` in a signature elides both, as before, but `Cx<'a>` written out no longer builds. The resolver `FileCx::new` borrows must outlive the `FileCx` and what holds it, so a walk that holds one declares its resolver before it. `FileCx` is generic over its resolver, `FileCx<'a, R>`, any of pdf-objects' `Resolve` (a closure among them, by value or by reference, or a `&Document` or `&Reading`), and `FileCx<'a>`, the default, names one over any resolver, a `dyn Resolve`, as a `Cx` reads through it: `&FileCx` in a signature takes a reference to one over any resolver, and one kept is kept by its resolver's type, or boxed (`Box<FileCx>`), as a closure's type can't be named. pdf-objects' `Resolve` is at its root, so a resolver of a type of its own, or a function over any, names it at the version pdf-lint takes, as it does `Unreadable` and `Why`, what `Resolve::try_resolve` says of an object that doesn't read. An object the resolver gives none for is null, whatever the reason: over a reading by position, one whose bytes the source failed to give, in that row and in the form's fields and the structure tree read then. pdf-structure's `Reading::failed_resolves` counts those, for a caller to learn that some were. `fn:InNameTree` alone asks why (`Resolve::try_resolve`), of each reference on the way to its name tree and in it, the row's own value aside: where one doesn't read, or a chain of references runs past those a value is followed through, the tree isn't read whole, and a key its readable part doesn't hold is unknown, not false. It's `Send` and `Sync` when its resolver is, as `&Document` and `&Reading` are, so one a file can be shared across threads. The `FileCx` keeps what the predicates read of the file as a whole once read, the name trees' keys, the structure tree's content items and the form's fields' names, which the caller held in `predicate::{Fields, Structure, Trees}`, now gone; a name tree is kept by where its root was written and the number of the object its entry names, not by where its root is in memory, and one known by neither is read each time. `fn:ImageIsStructContentItem` and `fn:PageContainsStructContentItems` know the objects the structure tree's content items name, and a row's container, by their numbers (`Cx::id` gives the container's), and one without a number by where it was written, not by where it is in memory: a container that can't be told apart from an item that could be it, as one built without its number, is unknown where it was false. `check` makes one `FileCx` a file, and gives each row the number of the indirect object its container is, when it's one. Every report is unchanged, but for a file whose object stream starts an object inside another's bytes, and whose structure tree names it: a page or image written directly at those bytes is now known as that object, where their places in memory told the two apart.
- **Breaking, the library: the model's lists have no fixed length, and its language can grow.** pdf-arlington's `predicate::SUPPORTED` and `extension::ISO` are `&[...]`, not arrays of a fixed length (0.1's were `[&str; 34]` and `[(&str, i64); 8]`), and `predicate::Expr` is `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a function or an extension can come without a breaking release. `Grammar::embedded()` gives the embedded model as a `&'static Grammar`, read once, where it gave an owned `Result<Grammar>` read anew at each call, and `predicate::TOLERANCE` is public. `extension::declared(&dyn Source)` stays this crate's, over pdf-arlington's, which takes the trailer's `/Root`, the packet's claims and a closure that resolves references, so pdf-arlington depends on pdf-objects' types alone. - **Breaking, the library: the model's types and its language's marked `#[non_exhaustive]`, and `ALL` a slice.** pdf-arlington's `ArlType` and `WrapFn`, and `predicate::Base` and `predicate::Arith` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, as the model and its language are upstream's to add to: a match on one takes a wildcard, and a type, a wrapping predicate, a path's start or an operator more is a minor release. `Row`, `Wrapped` and `Wrap`, and `grammar::Correction`, `grammar::Addition` and `predicate::Path` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, as each is read by its fields and never built by a caller: a literal of one no longer builds, a pattern of one takes `..`, and a field more is a minor release; `Table`, whose fields were never all public, is too. `ArlType::ALL` and `WrapFn::ALL` are `&[...]`, not arrays of a fixed length (0.1's were `[ArlType; 18]` and `[WrapFn; 5]`), each every variant once, in the order its enum lists them, so a type or a wrapping predicate can come without a breaking release. `At`, `Truth`, `Reference`, `Since`, `Gate` and `predicate::Cmp` stay exhaustive, on purpose, each one's doc saying why.
