# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

- **Tokens.** `tokens(bytes, &ResCx, &Limits)` gives a content stream's tokens in order, a `Tokens` iterator (fused, `Clone`, `Send` and `Sync`) of each token's range and its `Tok`: `Op`, an operator, any word that isn't a number, `true`, `false` or `null`; `Operand`, an operand's bytes as written, a number, a name, a string, an array or a dictionary as far as PDFium reads it (below), or a closing delimiter with nothing to close; or `InlineImage`, `BI` to `EI`, one token, with its data's range. Between them is only white-space and comments, and after an error nothing comes. Tokens end where PDFium's `CPDF_StreamParser` ends them: at white-space and delimiters, a word of digits, signs and points a number, strings nested, and an array or a dictionary one token, ended where PDFium ends it (below).
- **On pdf-tokenizer.** Content's tokens are pdf-tokenizer's (0.1.1), read by its PDFium rules (`Pdfium`), where they were a scanner of this crate's own: white-space and comments passed over without a token each (its `next_significant`), an array passed over by its `skim`, which reads only the tokens that open or close one and a string that doesn't close, where PDFium judges nothing of the rest (what's open kept on the heap, under the cap on depth, as before; and an array or a dictionary ended where PDFium ends it, below), operands, an image's dictionary and what a page's joins divide read from its tokens, and its byte classes, its number words' kinds and ISO 32000-2's number test (`decode::number`, `decode::integer`) its own. An operand's number, name and string values read by pdf-tokenizer's PDFium readings (`decode::pdfium`), as the entry after next says. An inline image's data is still found in its bytes where its filter ends it, or at an `EI` between white-space, each marker (`EI`, ASCIIHex's `>`, a JPEG's end of image) searched for with memchr, its default features off. The same tokens, operations, operands, errors and faults as the scanner gave, but in two places, each where the scanner read otherwise than PDFium: 0x80 and 0xFF are white-space, as PDFium reads them (it fills `1 2 3 4<80>re f`'s rectangle, takes `/F0<80>10 Tf`'s font `F0`, and takes either after `ID` as the one white-space byte before an inline image's data), where the scanner read them as regular bytes, part of a word or a name; where ISO 32000-2 reads a regular character they're told as `Fault::HighByte` (below), and as the byte after `ID` as `AfterId` alone, which needs ISO's white-space. And a word of more digits than 64 bits hold with a sign after them, such as `99999999999999999999--5`, no longer reads as the largest integer of its sign (Rust's integer parse stopped at the overflow, before the sign): it's a number word, which `object` reads as PDFium does, 0 (below), ISO 32000-2 writing no such number (§7.3.3).
