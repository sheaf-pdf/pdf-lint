# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

- **The handler.** `Handler::open` takes a `Dictionary`, the encryption dictionary's values typed, the file's first `/ID` element and passwords, and opens it by the first that's the owner's or the user's (or the empty password, given none): revisions 2 to 4 (Algorithms 2 to 7), 5 (Adobe's Extension Level 3) and 6 (Algorithms 2.A, 2.B, 11, 12), with `/Perms` checked (Algorithm 13). Short `/O` and `/U` are filled out with zeros, revision 5's and 6's long ones cut short, a `/V 2` key's `/Length` taken in 40 to 128 bits, as qpdf reads them; refused, saying why, are another handler, a version or revision it hasn't, `/O` or `/U` missing or too long, and no password that opens it.
- **Decrypting.** `decrypt_string` and `decrypt_stream` decrypt for object `n g` by its crypt filter (`/StmF`, `/StrF`, a stream's own by `named`): RC4 and AES-128 with each object's key (Algorithm 1), AES-256 with the file's (1.A). An `Error` has what decrypted, if anything, and its `Fault`: AES ciphertext with no whole block past its IV, a part block past the whole ones (white-space there, as a recovered `/Length` leaves, is left off without a word), padding that isn't, a crypt filter's method that isn't one, an AES key of the wrong length; and `Handler::faults`, `/Perms` that doesn't match.
- **A writer for tests** (the `writer` feature): an encryption dictionary for a user's and an owner's password of each kind (Algorithms 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10), and strings and streams encrypted, with salts, keys and IVs given.
- **Tests.** Each kind qpdf writes, opened by either password with qpdf's own key, its strings decrypted; the writer's, each way; each fault and refusal; and a fuzz target, `just fuzz crypt`, on dictionaries of any fields and on the writer's that open, whose body a unit test runs.
- **Primitives.** MD5 (RFC 1321), SHA-256, SHA-384 and SHA-512 (FIPS 180-4), RC4, and AES-128 and AES-256 (FIPS 197), encrypting and decrypting a block, and in CBC (SP 800-38A), all of the crate's own: checked against each standard's examples, against Python's hashlib at the lengths where padding changes, and against RustCrypto by a fuzz target in the repository's fuzz crate (`just fuzz primitives`).
