# Changelog

## 0.1.0 (unreleased)

- **The handler.** `Handler::open` takes a `Dictionary`, the encryption dictionary's values typed, the file's first `/ID` element and passwords, each an `Encoded` (below), and opens it by the first that's the owner's or the user's (or the empty password, given none): revisions 2 to 4 (Algorithms 2 to 7), 5 (Adobe's Extension Level 3) and 6 (Algorithms 2.A, 2.B, 11, 12), with `/Perms` checked (Algorithm 13). It tries those whose encoding fits the dictionary's revision first, the rest after, each in the order given; one whose bytes the revision takes as it took one tried before (padded or cut to 32 bytes for 2 to 4, cut to 127 for 5 and 6) isn't tried again, as it gives the same key, and for 5 and 6 costs no hash. `Handler::opened_by` says which opened it: its index among those given and its encoding, none for the empty password tried as none was given. Short `/O` and `/U` are filled out with zeros, revision 5's and 6's long ones cut short, a `/V 2` key's `/Length` taken in 40 to 128 bits, as qpdf reads them; refused, saying why, are another handler, a version or revision it hasn't, `/O` or `/U` missing or too long, and no password that opens it. `Dictionary` and `CryptFilter` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a field can come without a breaking release: outside the crate, one is made from `default()`, its fields set after.
- **Decrypting.** `decrypt_string` and `decrypt_stream` decrypt for object `n g` by its crypt filter (`/StmF`, `/StrF`, a stream's own by `named`): RC4 and AES-128 with each object's key (Algorithm 1), AES-256 with the file's (1.A). An `Error` has what decrypted, if anything, and its `Fault`: AES ciphertext with no whole block past its IV, a part block past the whole ones (white-space there, as a recovered `/Length` leaves, is left off without a word), padding that isn't, a crypt filter's method that isn't one, an AES key of the wrong length; and `Handler::faults`, `/Perms` that doesn't match.
- **A password as typed, and the bytes tried.** `Password` is a password's text; `Password::encodings` gives the bytes it may have been set as, each an `Encoded`, tagged with its `Encoding`: its SASLprep form (RFC 4013), in UTF-8, which revisions 5 and 6 take (ISO 32000-2, 7.6.4.3.3), where that isn't the text as typed; in UTF-8, as typed, as writers that skip SASLprep set it; then, for revisions 2 to 4, the text composed (NFC, so an accent typed apart from its letter has a byte) in PDFDocEncoding, WinAnsiEncoding and MacRomanEncoding (Annex D), each where every character has a byte, as qpdf tries them; none twice among those a revision takes first. A password SASLprep refuses has the rest alone. `Encoded::verbatim` takes bytes as they are. An `Encoded` is wiped when it's dropped, and neither its `Debug` nor a `Password`'s says the password. `Encoding::fits` says which revisions an encoding's passwords can have been set for: SASLprep's 5 and 6, the single-byte ones 2 to 4, UTF-8 and bytes as given any. Every form pdf-lint's `text::passwords` gave, `encodings` gives in the same order (a test holds the three tables' characters to being their own NFC, alone and in pairs), SASLprep's before them.
- **A new file's password, strictly.** `Password::prepared` gives its SASLprep form, tagged `Encoding::Saslprep`, of 1 to 127 bytes; or `Unusable`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, saying why it can't be one: `Prohibited`, a character SASLprep doesn't allow, the first of the typed text's that it refuses whatever is around it (stringprep's error says no character but of the text it made); `Bidi`, right-to-left text it doesn't allow; `TooLong`, over 127 bytes, which a reader would cut; `Vanished`, a password that prepares to nothing; `Empty`; and `Unprepared`, bytes not in SASLprep's form, for a new file's password given in another encoding.
- **A new file's encryption.** `Handler::create` makes a revision 6 encryption dictionary (`/V 5 /R 6`, AES-256 by the crypt filter `/StdCF`, `/CFM /AESV3`) for a password prepared, by Algorithms 8, 9 and 10, and the handler that encrypts under it: every permission granted (`/P -4`), the owner's password the user's, `/EncryptMetadata` as asked; the handler the owner's, `opened_by` none. Refused, nothing drawn (`Unusable`): a password not in SASLprep's form (`Unprepared`), and SASLprep's form that's empty (`Empty`) or over 127 bytes (`TooLong`), as `encodings` may give it. The file's key, the user's salts, the owner's and `/Perms`' random bytes are drawn in that order from a random number generator the caller passes, rand_core's `CryptoRng`, behind `dyn CryptoRng` or not, so that pdf-crypt has no randomness of its own and a seeded generator gives the same dictionary each time. The dictionary is whole as it's given: a crypt filter's `/AuthEvent` and `/Length`, which `CryptFilter` has no fields for, are the writer's to write or leave out.
- **rand_core re-exported** (`pdf_crypt::rand_core`), 0.10, the version RustCrypto's crates under it take, so that a caller names the `CryptoRng` `create` takes without a dependency of its own. It has no dependencies, and builds with Rust 1.85.
- **Encrypting for a writer.** `Handler::encrypt_string` and `encrypt_stream` encrypt as `decrypt_string` and `decrypt_stream` decrypt: by the crypt filter strings or streams are encrypted with, or a stream's own (`named`'s method). AES's IV is the first 16 bytes of HMAC-SHA256 (RustCrypto's `hmac`), under the file's key, of a context's length (8 bytes, big-endian), the context, which is the caller's name for the object, and the data: deterministic, so the same object encrypts to the same bytes on every save, and no IV serves two plaintexts. RC4 takes no IV, `/Identity` and `/None` leave the data as it is, and a method that isn't one is refused (`Fault::Method`). The MAC is keyed once, when the handler opens; `Handler` is `Clone`, `Send` and `Sync`, and its `Debug` leaves the MAC out with the key.
- **Keys kept in, and wiped.** Neither the file's key nor an object's is public, and `Handler`'s `Debug` leaves the key out. The file's key, each object's, and every buffer a password's or a key's bytes pass through on the way to one (the padded password, the MD5 and SHA-2 inputs built from it, the intermediate keys and digests, RC4's round keys, revision 6's rounds) are held in `zeroize`'s `Zeroizing`, sized where they're made, and wiped when dropped. AES keeps a key's schedule in place of the key, made once a key, and a handler of `/V` 5, whose AES strings and streams are all under the file's key, keys it once, when it opens. RustCrypto's `aes`, `cbc`, `md-5`, `rc4`, `sha2` and `hmac` are built with their `zeroize` features, which wipe their key schedules and hashing state. What it can't reach, the README says.
- **Errors, and room to grow.** `Method`, `Refused`, `Fault` and `Unusable` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, so a method, a refusal, a fault or a reason can come without a breaking release. `Refused`, `Fault` and `Error` derive `Display` and `std::error::Error` with thiserror; `Error` says its fault's text, with no `source`.
- **A writer for tests** (the `writer` feature): an encryption dictionary for a user's and an owner's password of each kind (Algorithms 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10; revisions 5 and 6 by the code `create` uses), and strings and streams encrypted, with salts, keys and IVs given. Its encryptors refuse a crypt filter the dictionary hasn't, or whose method isn't one (`Fault::Method`), as the public ones and decrypting do.
- **Tests.** Each kind qpdf writes, opened by either password with qpdf's own key, its strings decrypted; the writer's, each way; each fault and refusal; RFC 4013's SASLprep examples and RFC 4231's HMAC-SHA256 cases; a new file's dictionary opened by its password both ways, drawn in order, its bytes as an implementation of the algorithms apart gives them for a seeded generator's draws; and a fuzz target, `just fuzz crypt`, on dictionaries of any fields and on the writer's that open, on new files' dictionaries for any password, and on a password's forms and prepared form for any text, whose bodies three unit tests run.
- **Primitives.** RustCrypto's: MD5 (`md-5`), SHA-256, SHA-384 and SHA-512 (`sha2`), HMAC-SHA256 (`hmac`), RC4 (`rc4`), and AES-128 and AES-256 (`aes`, in CBC by `cbc`). Rust 1.85 builds it with `aes` 0.9.2 and `sha2` 0.11.0, which Cargo's resolver picks for that Rust when it minds Rust versions (resolver 3, edition 2024's); their later releases need a later Rust, and a project resolved without regard to it can hold them back with `cargo update -p aes --precise 0.9.2` and `-p sha2 --precise 0.11.0`. `hmac` 0.13 needs `digest`'s `mac`, which brings RustCrypto's `ctutils` and `cmov`; Rust 1.85 builds `ctutils` 0.4.2, which the resolver picks likewise, and 0.4.3 needs 1.87 (`cargo update -p ctutils --precise 0.4.2` holds it back).
- **SASLprep by `stringprep`** 0.1.5, with `unicode-normalization` 0.1 (the tables it composes the single-byte forms' text by), which bring `unicode-bidi`, `unicode-properties` and `tinyvec`; each builds with Rust 1.85. `unicode-normalization` 0.1.26 is `(MIT OR Apache-2.0) AND Unicode-3.0`, for Unicode's tables, a permissive licence. Its normalization is current Unicode's, not 3.2's, as the README says.
