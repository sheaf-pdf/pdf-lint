# Changelog

## 0.1.0-alpha.1 (unreleased)

The first release, and an alpha. Its own unit tests don't build from its package, as they read files through pdf-structure, which isn't published yet: a dev-dependency by its path alone, which the package's manifest leaves out, so the crate packages and publishes on pdf-objects alone.

- **Dates.** `date::Date` (ISO 32000-2, 7.9.4), its fields private so that it always writes as a date: `Date::read` reads one, or gives a `date::Error` (`#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror): `NoPrefix`, `NoYear`, or `OutOfRange` naming its `Field`. A field not written is none (`month`, `day`, `hour`, `minute`, `second`, `offset`), and `month_or_default` and the like give 7.9.4's default for it. `Offset` is `Utc`, `Ahead` or `Behind`. `Date::quirks` says what a reading read past, a `Quirk` (`#[non_exhaustive]`) each: hours without a sign, an offset before the fields it follows (whose fields then take their defaults), `Z` with an offset other than zero, an apostrophe where it doesn't belong or missing, 1.7's terminating apostrophe, a lone digit, what follows the date, and a day its month hasn't.
- **Ids are pdf-objects' struct.** `Page::id`, `Origin::Ancestor`, `Place::object`, each `Fault`'s id and the maps a resolver is built from hold pdf-objects' `ObjectId`, a struct of `number` and `generation`, where it was the pair `(u32, u16)`: `(1, 0)` is `ObjectId::new(1, 0)`, `id.0` is `id.number`.
- **`Place` and `Sink` are pdf-objects'.** A `Fault` is told to pdf-objects' `fault::Sink<Fault>` with its `fault::Place`, the types pdf-color's and pdf-fonts' faults are told to, so one sink can take all three; `Place` and `Sink` are no longer this crate's, nor at its root. pdf-objects is re-exported whole (`pdf_semantics::pdf_objects`), so a sink of one's own names them at the version this crate takes. Code follows: `use pdf_semantics::{Place, Sink}` is `use pdf_objects::fault::{Place, Sink}`, or `pdf_semantics::pdf_objects::fault`, `impl Sink for S` is `impl Sink<pdf_semantics::Fault> for S`, a bound `S: Sink` is `S: Sink<Fault>` and `&mut dyn Sink` is `&mut dyn Sink<Fault>`. A `Vec<(Place, Fault)>` is a sink now, keeping each with its place.
- **Dates written and built.** `Date::write` writes 7.9.4's form of the fields, never the bytes read, so reading it back gives the same fields and no quirk but a day its month hasn't, which is in the clause's range and is kept. `Date::new` and `with_month`, `with_day`, `with_hour`, `with_minute`, `with_second` and `with_offset` build one, each in its range and after the field before it (`Error::OutOfOrder`), a day being one its month has. A date read with a day its month hasn't keeps saying so through `with_hour` and the rest, which ask only of the fields they set.
- **The `clock` feature,** on by default, on chrono without its default features: `Date::from_unix`, a count of seconds as a date in UT, none before year 0 or past 9999, and `Date::now`. A crate that only reads and writes dates leaves it out, and chrono with it.
- **Name and number trees.** `tree::names` and `tree::numbers` (ISO 32000-2, 7.9.6 and 7.9.7), iterators over a tree's entries in the order it's written, a node's own then its kids', each key and value borrowed from what the resolver keeps, the value as written, reading only what's asked for. Each node or array reached by reference is read once, so a loop ends and says so (`Fault::TreeRepeated`). `Names::whole` and `Numbers::whole` say whether anything was left unread: a node past `Limits::tree_depth` or `Limits::tree_nodes`, a kid that's no dictionary, an entry that isn't an array, a key unread or of another type, an odd last key, an object that doesn't read. `into_sink` gives the sink back.
- **Trees written.** `tree::write_names` and `tree::write_numbers`: one root holding every entry, sorted, a key given twice or a value that's a stream or holds one refused (`tree::Error`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror).
- **Faults.** `Fault` (`#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror), one enum for the crate, owning nothing, each variant naming its clause, told to a sink with its place, the object it's in and where it was written (pdf-objects' `fault::Sink` and `fault::Place`, above), `()` dropping them and wanting none, so what only a checker wants isn't read: a tree's `/Limits` and the order of its keys are looked at only for a sink that wants faults, and what's read is the same whatever the sink; what only a check reads that doesn't read past a cap or for a password is `Fault::Unchecked`, the object and why, and the walk goes on.
- **The page tree.** `pages::pages` (ISO 32000-2, 7.7.3), every page in order over any resolver, by pdf-tokenizer's `Rules::Pdfium` or `Rules::Iso` (`pdf_semantics::pdf_objects::pdf_tokenizer::Rules`), into `pages::Pages` (`len`, `get`, `iter`, `counted`); and `pages::counted`, the count PDFium gives where its rules find no page under the root, which `pages` makes `Error::NoPages` though PDFium counts the root's `/Count`. A `Page` (`#[non_exhaustive]`) has its `id` and its `dict`, each none where it can be (written in place, or a place where PDFium loads no page), its `repeat`, its numbered `parents` (`pages::Parents`, an iterator of their ids, nearest first), each `Inheritable` as an `Attribute`, as written and followed, and `media_box`, `crop_box`, `rotate` and `user_unit` as `Sourced` values (`Rect`, degrees as written, a number), each with its `Origin`: its own, an ancestor's, or a default. `Attribute`, `Sourced` and `Origin` are `#[non_exhaustive]`. Read as of chromium/8125 by `Rules::Pdfium`: depth first, a kid with `/Kids` a node whatever its `/Type`, one written in place read as any other, one that names no dictionary a place with no page at it, the count PDFium gives by the root's `/Count` or a count of the tree that trusts a node's, and references followed as far as PDFium does, which is two for `/Pages`, `/Parent` and `/Rotate` and one otherwise. A node listed inside itself, a `/Parent` chain that loops and a tree past the caps are typed errors there, where PDFium goes on quietly (`pages::Error`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror). `Rules::Iso` never stops at a `/Count`, `/Type` telling a node from a page, null being absent, every chain followed, each departure said, and only the caps and a catalog with no `/Pages` an error.
- **Faults of a page tree.** `Fault::PageTree*` and `PageNoMediaBox`, each under ISO's rules and naming its clause; whatever costs a read, a `/Parent` against its holder and a `/Count` against what's under it, only for a sink that wants faults, so that whatever fails in them fails no walk: one that doesn't read is that fault, and one past a cap or a password `Fault::Unchecked`. `Fault::PageTreeCount { count, under }` holds the `/Count` as written where it's an integer and the pages the walk found under the node, so that a checker can tell an empty tree's 0 from a count that's wrong, and says both ("a node's /Count is 5, with 3 pages under it"); `Fault::PageTreeParent { lister }` holds the node that lists the page or node, none where that's written in place, which no `/Parent` can name ("a /Parent isn't 2 0 R, the node that lists it"). Each is built where it's told, from what the walk had already read, so a walk with `()` builds neither.
- **XMP claims.** `xmp::read` (ISO 32000-2, 14.3.2), on quick-xml with none of its features, reads a packet's `Claims` (`#[non_exhaustive]`), the first value of each, as written but for ASCII's white space round it: PDF/A's part, conformance and revision, PDF/UA's part and revision, and PDF/VT's version, each an attribute or an element under any prefix bound to its namespace. An attribute or a value that doesn't read is told (`Fault::XmpAttribute`, `Fault::XmpValue`) and passed, a claim elsewhere still counting; a mark of UTF-16 is told (`Fault::XmpNotUtf8`), and nothing's read. An `xmp::Error` (`#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror) holds what was read, where it stopped and why (`XmpWhy`): XML that stops making sense, or a cap. No DOCTYPE is read and no entity expanded, and quick-xml's types aren't in the public API. `Claims::conformance` reads PDF/A's and PDF/UA's as `Conformance`, `PdfA` and `PdfUa`, each `#[non_exhaustive]` and made by `new`, or a `ClaimError` naming the claim and the value; `PdfA` displays as `PDF/A-2b`, `PdfUa` as `PDF/UA-1`. An `&` that starts no reference ends nothing: passed in the packet's text, and in a claim's value told as `Fault::XmpValue`. Given the stream it's the data of, as `metadata_stream` says, `read` places each fault in that stream's decoded data. `xmp::metadata_stream` says which stream a catalog's `/Metadata` leads to, for a reader to decode, or why that doesn't read.
- **XMP read from a file, and written.** `xmp::read_from` reads a file's packet from the stream its catalog's `/Metadata` leads to, through pdf-objects' `Streams`, asking for its data under `Limits::xmp_bytes`: a packet past that, or data longer than was asked for, has nothing read (`XmpWhy::TooLong`), and what doesn't read on the way, or the data, is `XmpWhy::Unreadable`. `xmp::write` writes a packet of `Claims`, one element each in XMP's wrapper, that `xmp::read` reads back as they are, and refuses a value with white space round it or a character XML can't hold (`xmp::WriteError`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror).
- **A page tree written.** `pages::write` writes the nodes of a balanced tree over a caller's pages, in order: every page at one depth, under as few levels of nodes of at most a fanout's kids as hold them (`pages::FANOUT`, 32, or another; `usize::MAX` for one node), each node's kids as even as they can be, each node numbered by a function the caller gives, the root first, so a document's `new_id` serves. Each node is `/Type /Pages`, `/Parent` but the root, `/Kids` and `/Count`, with no inheritable attribute, and `pages::Written` (`#[non_exhaustive]`) has the root, the nodes and each page's `/Parent`. `pages::pages` reads it back by either rules with no fault under the same caps. No pages, a fanout under 2, a page given twice, object 0, an id already a page's or a node's and a tree past `Limits::pages` or `Limits::page_depth` are refused before an id is asked for, but an id refused as it's given (`pages::WriteError`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, thiserror).
- **Exhaustive on purpose:** `pages::Inheritable`, its four attributes listed by `Inheritable::ALL` with each one's key (`Inheritable::key`), and `date::Offset`, as their lists are closed and a consumer matches each. Every other enum, and every struct with public fields but `pages::Rect`, a value, is `#[non_exhaustive]`.
- **The `tracing` feature,** off by default: a span at `debug` over a page tree walked or counted (`pages::pages`, `pages::counted`), a packet read (`xmp::read`, and `xmp::read_from` around it) and a page tree written (`pages::write`), its fields numbers alone; none over a name or number tree's walk, whose loop is the caller's. Without it, nothing of them is compiled, and tracing isn't a dependency.
- **Caps.** `Limits` (`#[non_exhaustive]`), `Limits::DEFAULT`: a tree's depth, 32 levels below its root, and nodes, 100,000; a page tree's levels and `/Parent` chain, 1,024, and pages and visits to nodes, 1,048,574, with four times that of kids looked at, so that a kid giving neither, listed again and again, can't cost the square of a file; a packet's bytes, 16 MiB, levels, 256, at most 65,534, and namespaces bound at once, 128.
- **`follow` and `catalog`.** `follow`, a value's references followed under 32 hops, null none, as far as the resolver says; `catalog`, a trailer's `/Root` followed. A walk reads through `Resolve::try_resolve`: an object past a cap the reader reads under, that takes a password, or whose bytes the source didn't give ends it with an `Unreadable`; a damaged one is `Fault::Damaged`, and the walk goes on. A resolver is given by value, so `&reading`, `&document` or a closure, and a `&dyn Resolve` too. pdf-objects is re-exported whole and none of its items by name, so its types, `dict!`, its `text` module (text strings, 7.9.2.2) and pdf-tokenizer's `Rules` are named through `pdf_semantics::pdf_objects`, at the version this crate takes.
- **Tests.** Unit tests for each field's range at its edges, every quirk, each error, the order fields are set in, the writer's forms, every short string of a date's pieces read, written and read back, and seconds read as a date at the ends of the years a date can hold; for trees, each fault and what it does to `whole`, loops and repeats, each cap at its edge, what's read and when, each kind of failure at each place in a tree, each over a closure, a reading in memory or by position and a document, and written trees read back through the writer and parser; for the page tree, each rule of each set, what's inherited and from where, each kind of failure and the caps, over a reading in memory, one by position and a document, the same. `tests/pdfium`, in the repository and not packaged, holds the walk to PDFium's own, its answers recorded at chromium/8125, through a `pages` kind in the repository's oracle, with `differ.tsv` naming where it reads otherwise on purpose: a reference's generation, a node inside itself, a `/Parent` loop, a tree past the caps or none under its root, where `pages::counted` is held to PDFium's count. For XMP, every claim in each form, by its binding and not its prefix, the first value, what's trimmed, what's text, each fault, entities not expanded, a packet read through a reading of a file, plain and deflated, and each cap at its edge, what it keeps and how it's said.
- **A fuzz body and target** (`just fuzz semantics`): any bytes as objects, each tree walked the same whatever the sink, what a plain recursive walk gives at any depth, within a cap of nodes, and written, read back; its page tree, and each of its first dictionaries as a catalog, by each set of rules: the same whatever the sink, within the caps, what's said consistent, and the same pages by both where ISO's rules say nothing's wrong; any bytes as a date; any bytes as an XMP packet, the same whatever the sink, an error holding what it read, each value trimmed and what smaller caps read what the default ones read; any bytes as a file, its trees, pages and metadata stream the same over a reading and its document. Three stable unit tests run it.
